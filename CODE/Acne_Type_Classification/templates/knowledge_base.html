CLINICAL GUIDELINES

Acne Type & Face Region Analysis

An empirical guide to identifying acne types, their root physiological causes across different face regions, and suggested prevention remedies.

1. Cysts & Nodules

Severe

What is it? Large, painful, pus-filled acne lesions that form deep under the skin surface and are highly prone to permanent scarring.

Regional Breakdown:
Forehead Excess oil production, hormonal fluctuations, stress, or hair styling products blocking pores.
Cheeks Bacterial transfer from phone screens, dirty pillowcases, hands, or mechanical friction.
Chin & Jawline Hormonal activity (androgens), stress-induced cortisol spikes, and menstrual cycle triggers.
Nose Extremely oily area with large pores that accumulate thick sebum and plug follicles.
Recommended Care:

Requires clinical dermatologist attention. Recommended treatments include oral isotretinoin, prescription-strength topical applications, or hormonal therapies. Avoid popping or squeezing.

2. Pustules

Moderate

What is it? Red, inflamed skin bumps containing white or yellow pus near the surface of the skin.

Regional Breakdown:
Forehead Excess sebum combined with sweat accumulation under hats or hairlines.
Cheeks Transfer of bacteria from dirty fingers, makeup tools, or comedogenic cosmetics.
Chin Hormonal acne flare-ups or friction from resting chin on hands.
Nose Infection of clogged pores by acne-causing bacteria.
Recommended Care:

Use benzoyl peroxide washes, topical retinoids, or salicylic acid spot treatments. Keep the face clean and avoid heavy, oil-based foundations.

3. Papules

Mild to Mod

What is it? Small, firm, red inflamed bumps on the skin surface that do not contain visible pus.

Regional Breakdown:
Forehead Early stages of pore blockage caused by cellular debris and oil.
Cheeks Skin irritation, sweat accumulation, or friction from masks/clothing.
Jawline Hormonal signals swelling the sebaceous glands.
Nose Irritation of localized clogged follicles due to excessive sebum.
Recommended Care:

Cleanse gently without rubbing. Apply salicylic acid cleansers or topical retinoids (such as adapalene) at night. Soothe inflammation with niacinamide.

4. Whiteheads

Mild

What is it? Closed clogged pores where oil, keratin, and dead skin cells remain trapped beneath a thin layer of skin.

Regional Breakdown:
Forehead Trapped oil under dry dead cells. Common with dry skin or lack of exfoliation.
Nose Follicles clogged at the surface. Avoid extraction tools which expand pores.
Chin Hormonal shifts altering the thickness of natural oils.
Cheeks Pore-plugging ingredients from heavy night creams or cosmetic powders.
Recommended Care:

Over-the-counter salicylic acid or glycolic acid (AHAs) cleansers. Exfoliate regularly and hydrate with non-comedogenic gel moisturizers.

5. Blackheads

Mild

What is it? Open clogged pores where sebum and skin cells are exposed to air, oxidizing and turning dark brown or black.

Regional Breakdown:
Nose (Most Common) High density of sebaceous glands and large open pores prone to oxidation.
Forehead Oily T-zone accumulating oil and environmental debris in open follicles.
Chin Hormonal sebum accumulation in chin crevices.
Cheeks Environmental dust, makeup residue, and oil collecting in cheek pores.
Recommended Care:

Regular application of Salicylic Acid (BHA) to dissolve oil within the pores. Use clay masks weekly to absorb surface sebum. Avoid physical pore strips.

Face Region & Common Triggers

A simplified reference list mapping specific face regions to their primary everyday triggers.

Face Region Primary Everyday Causes
Forehead Excessive oil production, stress, sweat buildup, and hair care products (pomades/sprays) blocking pores.
Cheeks Dirty pillowcases, bacteria from cell phone screens, touching face with unwashed hands, and thick cosmetics.
Nose Concentration of large oil pores, excess natural oil production, and dead skin buildup on the middle face area.
Chin Hormonal changes (especially stress or cycle hormones), dietary choices, and stress.
Jawline Hormonal cycles, internal chemistry shifts, and friction from chin straps, masks, or tight collars.
Around Eyebrows Hair treatments, sweat running down the forehead, and oil gland buildup near the brows.
Around Mouth Irritating food residue, lip balms with heavy waxes, toothpaste sensitivity, and hormonal shifts.
